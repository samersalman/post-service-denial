1. STUDY QUESTION AND DESIGN
1.1 Ecological, plan-level cross-sectional study of 2024 in-network post-service claim denial in Individual-market Marketplace QHPs, in relation to orthopaedic surgeon network density. Unit of analysis: plan. Clustering unit: issuer. Reporting: STROBE with RECORD where applicable.
1.2 Single primary hypothesis: higher orthopaedic surgeon network density is associated with a lower in-network denial proportion. Two-sided test, alpha 0.05, 95 percent confidence intervals for every estimate. No other threshold language is used.
1.3 The outcome covers all specialties, not orthopaedics. The paper must not claim an orthopaedic denial rate.

2. ANALYTIC SAMPLE
2.1 Cohort: the 2,527 plans (149 issuers, 30 states) with a computable in-network denial proportion; cohort membership is the row set of v1/data_clean/ortho/plan_exposure.csv.
2.2 Exposure set (enrollment-free): exposure_status ok, 2,175 plans, 136 issuers, 30 states (86.1 percent of the cohort).
2.3 Analytic sample (primary and most sensitivity rows): exposure_status ok AND numeric reported average monthly enrollment (complete case), 1,681 plans, 135 issuers, 30 states, 135 issuer-states (66.5 percent of the cohort). Issuer IDs are state specific, so issuers and issuer-states are the same units.
2.4 Ladder reported in Table 1 (plans; issuers): cohort 2,527; 149. Excluded for exposure status 352 plans: issuer_unreachable 263 (11 issuers), issuer_incomplete 77 (2 issuers, BCBS Florida, 8 of 52 provider shards harvested), plan_not_in_directory 8 (2 issuers), no_individuals 4 (2 issuers). Exposure ok 2,175; 136. Of these, 494 plans (81 issuers, 27 states) have a "**" enrollment and leave the analytic sample; every non-numeric enrollment among exposure-ok plans is "**". Analytic 1,681; 135. Numeric enrollment exists for 1,942 cohort plans (148 issuers); 261 of them are outside the exposure set. Table 1 also reports in-network claim lines received for every rung; WP6b computes them (they are an outcome-side denominator, not an output of the blind diagnostics).
2.5 Structural comparison (no outcome): analytic plans differ from excluded cohort plans in plan type (HMO 51.9 versus 38.2 percent, POS 11.2 versus 18.8 percent), metal level (Catastrophic 0.8 versus 7.3 percent) and state (Texas 27.7 versus 18.2 percent, Florida 5.4 versus 14.4 percent, North Carolina 3.0 versus 13.1 percent). Reported enrollment is similar among plans that have it (median 97, quartiles 37 and 276, versus median 90, quartiles 27 and 336). See sap_check_included_excluded*.csv.
2.6 Selection check, done by WP6b after the freeze and descriptive only: the denial proportion and the claim volume (in-network claim lines received) of analytic versus excluded cohort plans, and of exposure-ok versus unreachable-issuer plans. The unreachable set is not random.
2.7 Plans whose exposure is not ok are excluded from every exposure analysis. No imputation of exposure, except the directory-network inheritance in 9.4.

3. OUTCOME
3.1 Plan-level in-network denial proportion: denied claim lines divided by received claim lines (2024 dates of service, Individual QHP, post-service, final adjudication; a claim is a service line). Modelled as grouped binomial counts, response cbind(denied, received - denied), using reported numeric cells only.
3.2 Out-of-network is descriptive only and is not modelled; rows with out-of-network denied above received are excluded from out-of-network rates.

4. PRIMARY EXPOSURE
4.1 Orthopaedic surgeons per 10,000 reported average monthly enrollees in the plan's directory network. Numerator: n_ortho_surgeon_text from the frozen text crosswalk (class ortho_surgeon; physician assistant, nurse practitioner and APRN co-labelled records are NOT in it; each NPI counted once per plan). Denominator: the plan's reported average monthly enrollment. density = n_ortho_surgeon_text / enrollment x 10,000. Label "per 10,000 reported enrollees" everywhere.
4.2 Scale: log2(density), so the odds ratio is per DOUBLING of density.
4.3 Zero rule: log2(density + c), c = half the smallest positive density, only for an exposure that has an exact zero in the analytic sample. Primary exposure: no exact zero (minimum surgeon count 7), no offset (design_zero_offset_needed 0). Dermatology: no zero, no offset. Ophthalmology: 5 analytic plans have a zero count, so the control uses c = 49.1096 (design_zero_offset_ophth), fixed now and used in every sample.
4.4 Plain description of the distribution (analytic sample, density per 10,000 reported enrollees): minimum 10.7, 5th percentile 2,545, 25th 14,663, median 46,752, 75th 157,381, 95th 862,174, maximum 1,910,909. Surgeon count: minimum 7, 5th percentile 91, 25th 245, median 508, 75th 1,690, 95th 1,983, maximum 2,407. Reported enrollment: minimum 11, median 97, 95th percentile 1,563. The median density is about 4.7 listed surgeons per reported enrollee. It is not plausible as a population density: it divides a directory-wide network count (the directory lists each issuer's full published network, which can include out-of-state clinicians; no address was retained) by the reported enrollment of a single plan. It is a relative measure only.

5. SECONDARY EXPOSURES (Holm family, size 2)
5.1 (i) Surgeon share of listed network clinicians: n_ortho_surgeon_text / n_individual_npis, on the log2 scale, on all exposure-ok plans (enrollment-free; 2,175 plans). Median 1.34 percent (5th to 95th percentile 0.35 to 3.80 percent). Covariates as in 7.2 but without the enrollment term.
5.2 (ii) APC-inclusive density: (n_ortho_surgeon_text + n_ortho_apc_text) / enrollment x 10,000, log2, analytic sample, same model as the primary. It correlates 0.997 (Spearman, log2 scale) with the primary density, so it is expected to repeat the primary estimate.
5.3 Holm adjustment applies to the two Wald P values of (i) and (ii) only (stats::p.adjust, method holm). The primary is not adjusted. Intervals are not adjusted. Family size is design_holm_family_size = 2.
5.4 Subspecialty completeness (0 to 7 represented subspecialties) is DROPPED: the NPPES bulk file stayed offline (HTTP 404 at the start and end of the harvest and again at this freeze) and the directory text rarely names a subspecialty. It appears in the original plan only as a pre-specified secondary that could not be built. If NPPES returned, the lead decides before any outcome is joined.

6. NEGATIVE CONTROLS (outside the Holm family)
6.1 Dermatology density (n_derm_text, class derm, NUCC 207N family pooled) and ophthalmology density (n_ophth_text, class ophth, 207W family pooled), same definition, scale, sample and model as the primary.
6.2 Reading rule, fixed now. It is applied only if the 95 percent CI of the primary odds ratio excludes 1. If a control estimate has the same sign as the orthopaedic estimate on the log-odds scale and a point estimate at least half as large (design_control_reading_ratio 0.5), the paper states the association is not specific to orthopaedics. If the control is smaller or opposite in sign, the paper says the association was not reproduced for that specialty, without claiming specificity. If the primary CI includes 1, the controls are reported without the rule.
6.3 Measurement context: PPV of each text flag against NPI Registry taxonomy codes (design_ppv_derm 92 percent, Wilson 81 to 97; design_ppv_ophth 98 percent, 90 to 100).
6.4 Correlations between the exposures, reported in the paper beside rows sens_g and sens_h. Raw Spearman correlations of the log2 densities with the orthopaedic density: 0.983 (dermatology) and 0.839 (ophthalmology, 1,676 plans with a positive count); this is largely the shared enrollment denominator. Within-issuer partial correlations, after removing log2 enrollment, plan type, metal group and issuer effects (the terms of the primary model; exposure only): 0.961 for dermatology and 0.784 for ophthalmology (0.753 among the 1,676 plans with a positive ophthalmology count); keys design_partial_corr_derm and design_partial_corr_ophth. The dermatology control therefore mostly tracks network breadth, so a same-sign control estimate cannot be read as a lack of specificity for orthopaedics. Section 6.2 stays as written; the 6.2 statement is worded as a description of the control estimate (same sign and at least half as large, or not), is always accompanied by the two correlations, and is not offered as a finding about specificity or non-specificity on the dermatology row alone.

7. PRIMARY MODEL
7.1 Fit: stats::glm, family quasibinomial (logit), response cbind(denied, received - denied), analytic sample.
7.2 Terms: exposure log2(density); log2 reported enrollment (decided from the diagnostics in Section 16: plan size carries most of the variation in density, so without this term the estimate is mainly a plan-size contrast); plan type (EPO, HMO, POS, PPO; reference HMO); metal level pooled to three groups (Bronze with Catastrophic, Silver, Gold with Platinum; reference Bronze); plus fixed effects as set by 7.4. With the enrollment term in the model, the exposure coefficient equals the coefficient of log2 surgeon count at fixed reported enrollment, so it is unchanged by a multiplicative scale error in reported enrollment that is common to all plans (or, given issuer fixed effects, common within an issuer). Metal levels with fewer than design_metal_pool_min_plans = 20 analytic plans are pooled (Catastrophic 14 plans, Platinum 9); the pooling is used in every model whatever its sample.
7.3 Standard errors: sandwich::vcovCL, type "HC1", cluster = issuer. Wald z intervals (normal quantile 1.96), no further small-sample claims. Report the odds ratio per doubling with its 95 percent CI. If any coefficient is aliased, remove the aliased columns, refit, then compute the covariance; log the removal. An aliased exposure coefficient counts as non-convergence. A second, conservative interval is pre-specified: the same estimate and HC1 covariance with a t quantile on K - 1 = 32 degrees of freedom (design_t_interval_df; row sens_t). The primary CI stays the z interval above; Methods report the t interval in one sentence.
7.4 Decision rule, fixed from Section 16. An INFORMATIVE ISSUER has at least 2 analytic plans and at least 2 distinct values of n_ortho_surgeon_text among them. Let K be their number. If K >= 30 (design_informative_issuer_threshold) the primary model has issuer fixed effects (the within-issuer comparison). If K < 30 it has state fixed effects instead, and the issuer fixed effects model becomes a sensitivity row with K stated. Computed result: K = 33 (129 issuers have 2 or more analytic plans), so the primary model is the ISSUER FIXED EFFECTS model (design_primary_model = issuer_fe). The margin is 3 issuers. The 33 informative issuers hold 907 plans in 19 states. K counts issuers; it is not a measure of effective information. Identifying variation is concentrated (Section 16.9): the residual variation of the primary exposure, after log2 enrollment, plan type, metal group and issuer effects, is carried 31.0 percent by issuer 46944 (Alabama, 12 plans, 3 distinct counts), 20.6 percent by issuer 33602 (Texas, 380 plans, 2 distinct counts; it also holds 41.9 percent of the plans in informative issuers) and 10.3 percent by issuer 38166 (Wisconsin, 18 plans), 62.0 percent together. The effective number of issuers (inverse Herfindahl of these shares, not weighted by claims) is 6.2; a claims-weighted effective number is likely lower. Row sens_d removes issuer 46944, row sens_r removes issuer 33602, row sens_s refits without each informative issuer in turn and row sens_t gives the t interval. The same model type is used for every exposure; K is not recomputed per sensitivity row.
7.5 Fallback if a model fails to converge or the exposure is aliased: report the failure, then use, in order, the state fixed effects model, then the mixed model of Section 8. Covariates are never dropped.

8. SECONDARY MODEL
8.1 lme4::glmer, binomial, response cbind(denied, received - denied), same exposure and covariates as 7.2 but no issuer or state fixed effects, with random intercepts for state, issuer within state (state_issuer) and an observation-level plan effect (same structure as the WP6a null model, which converged). Use nAGQ = 0 if needed. Report the odds ratio per doubling with a Wald 95 percent CI.

11. MEASUREMENT ERROR
11.1 The surgeon-text flag has PPV 85 percent (Wilson 81 to 89) for any NUCC 207X code in a stratified NPI Registry sample (design_ppv_surgeon_text). Its sensitivity cannot be estimated reliably: with 0 events in 497 unflagged NPIs the Jeffreys-based Monte Carlo interval is about 65 to 99 percent, and the point value (93 percent) is prior-dependent. Report the interval, not the point value (ortho_sensitivity_surgeon and its limits in ledger_ortho.csv). Read the values from the ledger, not from older handoff text.
11.2 Handling: describe PPV and sensitivity; no quantitative correction of the main estimate; do not condition on the validation sample. One bounded statement in Discussion: a constant PPV does not change a log-scale slope; differential misclassification could.

12. SUPPRESSION
12.1 "**" is inferred to mean a count of 1 to 10 (the smallest positive count in any claim, reason, appeal or enrollment column is 11). Only "**" is bounded; "*", "***" and "N/A" are structural and never bounded. The primary analysis uses reported numeric cells only. Bounds 1, 5.5, 10 and 0 apply to rows sens_i to sens_l, sens_n and sens_o. KFF's replication rule (suppressed set to zero) appears only in the replication sentence.

13. ENROLLMENT LIMITATION
13.1 Reported average monthly enrollment is numeric for 1,942 of 2,527 cohort plans and appears about 10 times too small relative to claim volume (about 423 claim lines per reported enrollee; a reviewer finding taken from the lead's note, not recomputed in this outcome-blind step). The median analytic plan reports 97 enrollees against 508 listed surgeons. The enrollment term in the primary model and the issuer fixed effects make the exposure slope unchanged by a multiplicative scale error that is common within an issuer. The label stays "per 10,000 reported enrollees". Rows sens_a (no adjustment), sens_e (enrollment-free exposure) and sens_k, sens_l (bounded enrollment) bracket the issue.

14. LIMITS TO WRITE INTO METHODS AND DISCUSSION
- The outcome covers all specialties, not orthopaedics.
- Ecological, plan-level design; no claim-line or patient data.
- The directory is dated October 2026; the claims are from 2024.
- The plan-level file contains only plans continuing into 2026.
- Specialty is free text; physician assistants and nurse practitioners can carry an orthopaedic label; PPV 85 percent.
- The directory lists each issuer's full published network, possibly including out-of-state clinicians; no address was retained.
- Plans of one issuer often share one network. Of 129 issuers with 2 or more analytic plans, 96 have identical surgeon counts for all plans and 95 have a single network ID; K = 33. Within issuer, 6.8 percent of the variance of log2 density comes from log2 surgeon count, 90.9 percent from log2 reported enrollment and 2.3 percent from their covariance, so within-issuer exposure variation is mostly plan size (see 7.2).
- Identifying information is concentrated: K = 33 counts issuers and is not a measure of effective information. Three issuers (46944, 33602, 38166) carry 31.0, 20.6 and 10.3 percent of the residual variation of the primary exposure; the effective number of issuers is 6.2 (unweighted; a claims-weighted figure is likely lower). The z interval with 135 clusters is therefore likely too narrow; the t interval on 32 degrees of freedom (sens_t) and the leave-one-issuer-out range (sens_s) are reported beside it.
- 11 issuers with a cohort plan were unreachable (263 plans); BCBS Florida was incomplete (77 plans; 8 of 52 provider shards harvested, the other 44 not run because of host speed and the decoded-size backstop).
- Coverage margin: 2,175 of 2,527 cohort plans (86.1 percent) against the 85 percent threshold; the analytic sample is 66.5 percent.
- Reported enrollment is missing ("**") for 585 cohort plans and looks too small (13.1).
- Denial reasons are tallies, not unique claims; "**" is an inferred range.
- The text flag may misclassify non-differentially or differentially (11.2).

17. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES FOR WP6b
17.1 Variables. expo_ortho = log2(n_ortho_surgeon_text / enrollment x 10000); expo_apc, expo_derm, expo_ophth likewise (ophthalmology adds design_zero_offset_ophth inside the log); expo_share = log2(n_ortho_surgeon_text / n_individual_npis); lenroll = log2(enrollment). Factor levels: plan type reference HMO; metal3 reference Bronze; issuer for fixed effects = issuer_id; mixed-model issuer = paste(state, issuer_id, sep = "_").
17.2 Primary formula: cbind(denied, received - denied) ~ expo_ortho + lenroll + plan_type + metal3 + factor(issuer_id), quasibinomial. Odds ratio per doubling = exp(coefficient of expo_ortho).
17.3 Seeds: design_seed for anything random. Software versions are logged by WP6b.
17.4 Outputs WP6b must add for the paper: in-network claim lines received for each Table 1 rung; the claim-volume comparison of analytic versus excluded cohort plans (2.6); the number of plans added in sens_i, sens_j, sens_k, sens_l, sens_n and sens_o; for sens_s the full list of leave-out estimates; for sens_t the t interval and P value on 32 degrees of freedom.
